The challenge

Kakitangan.com is HR and payroll software for Malaysian businesses. One dashboard serves everyone: employees checking leave and payslips, managers checking who's in today, and HR admins clearing approvals and running payroll.

Early feedback was clear: it felt like too much information, admins wanted their approvals first, and payroll items were hard to find.

What I did

  • 🔍Planned and ran two rounds of user surveys with task-based tests
  • 🎨Designed the layout, cards and states for each role, on desktop and phone
  • 🧪Built clickable prototypes that switch between people, roles and company setups
  • 📝Wrote the product spec, acceptance criteria and edge cases
  • 🗺️Split the work into three releases and planned the first one
  • ✨Built the first release's changes, then handed off to engineering

Reading the dip: Round 2 asked people to complete real tasks, which usually lowers "easy" and "confident" scores. "Gets things done faster" held steady, and 64% preferred the improved version over the first (20%).

The weakest results were both about finding payroll things. That changed the plan: payslip access went into the first release, and missing bank details became a to-do item for admins in a later one.

Design decisions

What people said, and what changed

  1. "Admin approvals should come first" · 5 people

    Team approvals is now the first card for admins.

  2. "The attendance numbers are wrong when people work more than one shift"

    My team now follows each person's shift: who's working now, on a break, on half-day leave, or still on last night's shift.

  3. Only 48% could find their payslip

    Payroll admins get a direct "My payslip" link, and staff without a payslip see a clear message instead of an empty button.

  4. "Show my leave as 14/20" · "Pending claims show no amount"

    Leave tiles read "14/20 days available", and claims show amounts in the company's own currency.

  5. "I can't find Switch Companies"

    The company name sits in the greeting, with a clear way to switch or add a company.

Release plan

Ship the biggest fixes first

Instead of waiting for everything, I split the redesign into three releases so users get the most-requested fixes early.

  1. V1

    In review

    Fix what users asked for most

    Approvals first, shift-aware team view, payslip access, clearer leave and claim tiles.

  2. V2

    Planned

    Company announcements

    A place for HR to share news, asked for in both rounds.

  3. V3

    Planned

    Admin to-dos and reporting

    Things that need fixing (like missing bank details) and trends for admins.

How I worked

Prototypes people could actually use

Instead of static screens, I built clickable prototypes. Reviewers could switch between five people and roles, different company setups and tricky states, and try the real flow before any engineering time was spent.

I then built the first release's changes, backed by 174 automated tests and handoff notes covering the risks, data changes and how to test.

What I learned

  • Ratings alone looked good. Real tasks exposed the payroll gaps that ratings missed.
  • Not every request should be built. Some were kept as they are on purpose, with the reason written down.
  • Clickable prototypes made reviews faster, because people reacted to a real flow instead of imagining one.

What's next

  • Release V1, then re-run the same tasks to measure the change.
  • Test with phone-only users and real attendance data, which neither round covered.
  • Move the payslip higher up the page for everyone.
