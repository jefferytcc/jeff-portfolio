Leave that explains itself

Leave touches everyone in a company. Staff check balances and apply, managers and HR approve, and everyone wants to know who's away. I designed the new leave module as a clickable prototype, tested it against real-world setups, then built its pages with AI on the new platform.

Design decisions

What each page does

One queue for approvals

"Waiting on you" shows only what needs a decision, with the count up front. Search, filters and bulk select help HR clear a long list. Each row shows who, what and how long, with Decline and Approve side by side.

See who's away at a glance

In the month view, busier days are darker, so clashes stand out. Pick a day to see exactly who's away and why, filtered by department or location. Holidays show on the calendar too.

Made for phones too

Every page works at phone width. On small screens the calendar keeps its month grid, and tapping a day opens the full list of who's away.

Reports grouped the way HR thinks

Everyone's balances, grouped by department or location, with each group opening on demand so a large company stays readable. Switch to a person or a leave type, then export to Excel.

Reviewing my own work

Before handing over, I review the build like a new user would, on desktop and phone. The latest pass turned up these fixes for the next round.

  • Explain the numbers on the month calendar, so it's clear they count people away.
  • Keep leave-type colours apart from status colours, so a green or orange tag never reads as "approved" or "warning".
  • Make the reports table work on phones, not just scroll sideways.
  • Use the same date format everywhere, including date pickers.
