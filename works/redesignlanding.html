Case study · Kakitangan.com

Redesigning the Kakitangan.com website

The old website didn't explain why a business should choose Kakitangan.com. As designer and PM, I worked on its redesign around one question every SME owner asks, "what's in it for me?", with clearer messaging, trust signals and a page for every product.

The problem

A website that didn't build trust

The old website didn't communicate the brand or the full value of the product. Information was scattered, and it never made clear why a business should choose Kakitangan.com.

Customer success stories, feature explanations and a clear way to get started were missing. Visitors struggled to see the benefits, which hurt sign-ups and trust.

The approach

Clarity, trust and conversion

Together with Walk Production, we researched what SME owners need and how competitors position themselves, then rewrote the messaging around Kakitangan.com's strengths. The redesign focused on four things:

  • Clear message

    One promise and obvious calls to action for sign-ups and demos.

  • Trust signals

    Testimonials, partners and compliance, up front.

  • Mobile-friendly

    For business owners checking on the go.

  • Found and fast

    Better SEO and faster pages for stronger visibility.

Design decisions

What changed

  1. 01 · Hero

    A promise instead of a stock photo

    "Manage your people, all in one place" over a stock photo became a clear promise, two calls to action (Try for free, Get a quotation) and three reasons to trust it: secure and compliant, fully automated, and on-call support.

  2. 02 · Navigation

    Simpler menu, pricing up front

    Seven mixed links became four clear groups: Solutions, Pricing, Resources and Company, with one "Request demo" button. Pricing now sits in the main menu.

  3. 03 · Products

    Every product at a glance

    A single row shows every product, each with its own colour, so visitors can jump to what they came for: Payroll, Leave, Claims, Attendance and more.

  4. 04 · Product pages

    One template for every product

    Each product page follows the same pattern: a plain-language promise, the same two calls to action, then the main benefits. Easy to scan, and easy for the team to add new products.
