Everything about an employee, on one page

HR admins open an employee's profile many times a day. In the old page, related information sat in different places: employment history on its own page, next of kin under General, every leave type listed separately. The redesign brings it together into one profile with clear sections.

Edit

Each section has its own Edit button, which opens a pop-up with just that section's fields. People update one thing without scrolling through a long form.

Design decisions

What changed, and why

  • The essentials stay in view

    Name, role, status, contact details and actions sit in a side card that stays on screen whichever section you open.

  • Numbers people understand

    Length of service shows in years and months, not a count of days.

  • Contact in one click

    Email and phone open the mail or phone app, and can be copied straight to the clipboard.

  • Related things together

    Employment history joined Employment, next of kin joined Family, and every leave type sits in one view.

  • A calendar for off days

    Off days switched from a long list to a calendar, so patterns are easy to see.

  • A history you can trust

    The activity log shows what changed and who changed it. Notes keep context about the person in one place.

Permissions

One page, different views

The same profile looks different to HR, payroll, claims and attendance admins, managers and employees. For example, an HR admin without payroll access never sees the Compensation tab. Try it with the "Viewer can see payroll" switch above.

Account status

Actions that fit the situation

If an employee hasn't activated their account yet, the Actions menu offers to resend the invite email, so HR doesn't have to go looking for it elsewhere.

Working with engineering

Designed for the details that break things

  • Some companies have a lot of custom leave types, so the Leaves view had to work with many, not just the usual few.
  • Every field kept its original input type (drop-down, date picker or free text), so the redesign didn't break existing data.
  • The engineering team rebuilt the page section by section, with API docs and tests for each one.
